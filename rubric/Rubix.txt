2.1 Population rules (PROPOSED RULE)
------------------------------------
  - Application evidence: use only material available at application time (CV, application answers,
    portfolio as submitted). Record its date. Never back-fill it with information learned later.
  - Interview evidence: use only notes or scores recorded during the interview process, with the
    date and the assessor's role. Keep these separate from application evidence.
  - Reported performance: copy the rating verbatim, with the rating scale, date, period covered and
    rater role. "Meets Expectations" is recorded as satisfactory, never as failure.
  - Employment status: record only what is explicitly stated (for example "Active as of <date>",
    "Resigned <date>", "Terminated <date>"). If it is not stated, write "Not stated". Do not infer
    that someone stayed, left or was dismissed. A departure is not evidence of poor performance.
  - Tenure: record start and end or as-of dates. Treat outcomes after less than 6 months as
    Indeterminate unless a rating exists for that period (the 6-month threshold is configurable).
  - Source: filename plus heading or passage for every cell that contains a claim.

2.2 Outcome interpretation categories (PROPOSED RULE, pending founder definition of success, gap G3)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
+----------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Category       | Definition (performance-based; employment status is never used alone)             |
+================+===================================================================================+
| Stronger       | Rating above the satisfactory level, or explicitly documented outcomes exceeding  |
|                | role expectations (for example the JD 6-month success criteria, F4 / F7).         |
+----------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Satisfactory   | "Meets Expectations" or equivalent. Documented as meeting role expectations.      |
+----------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Weaker         | Rating below the satisfactory level, or explicitly documented performance         |
|                | concerns.                                                                         |
+----------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Indeterminate  | No rating, tenure too short, conflicting records, or only employment status       |
|                | available.                                                                        |
+----------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
Comparisons across hires must be made within the same role and seniority where possible. A stronger
outcome for, say, an engineer does not establish what predicts success for a PM. With the sample
sizes expected at a 40-person company, any comparison should be read as a hypothesis.

6. SCORING, COVERAGE, AND REVIEW RULES
====================================================================================================

Every rule in this section is a PROPOSED RULE. All are configurable operational conventions that
need calibration. None is a validated finding.

6.1 Scoring each criterion
--------------------------
  - Each criterion has a weight w (Sections 4-5). It receives a score s from 1 to 5 only when there
    is sufficient relevant evidence to match an anchor. Otherwise it is marked NE (Not Enough
    Evidence).
  - Missing evidence never receives 0, 1 or an assumed average. Absence of evidence is recorded as
    NE.
  - A low score (1-2) requires explicit negative evidence, meaning something the candidate said,
    wrote or did that matches the low anchor. Silence on a topic is NE, not 1.
  - [INTERVIEW/WORK-SAMPLE ONLY] criteria (PM-3, SPM-3) must be NE at CV-screen stage.
  - [CV-PARTIAL] criteria may be scored from a CV only on specific behavioral evidence. Candidate-
    evidence confidence is capped at Medium and the criterion must be re-scored after interview.
  - CV statements are claims. Treat them as evidence of what the candidate reports, and verify
    material claims in interview.
  - Score the evidence once, under the single criterion it best fits (the criterion notes explain
    boundaries). Do not re-use one achievement to lift several criteria unless it genuinely shows
    different behaviors, and note why.

6.2 Formulas
------------
  Evidence coverage (%)          = sum of w over scored criteria
  Provisional evidence score     = 100 x sum[ w x (s - 1) / 4 ]  /  sum[ w for scored criteria ]
  If no criterion is scored      -> "Not scorable"
  Display (always both):          "Score XX.X/100 at YY% coverage (stage: <stage>)"

Worked example using hypothetical numbers only, not a candidate. PM-1 s=4 (w20), PM-2 s=3 (w25),
PM-3 NE, PM-4 s=5 (w15), PM-5 NE, PM-6 s=2 (w10). Coverage = 20+25+15+10 = 70%. Score = 100 x
(20x0.75 + 25x0.50 + 15x1.00 + 10x0.25) / 70 = 100 x 45 / 70 = 64.3. Display: "Score 64.3/100 at 70%
coverage (stage: CV screen)".

6.3 Interpretation limits
-------------------------
  - The score is not a probability of success, and it has no historical validation (gap G1).
  - Coverage below 70% (a provisional minimum): do not interpret the total score. Recommend
    collecting more evidence or human review. The 70% level is an operational convention and must be
    calibrated. For reference, the maximum CV-stage coverage is 80% for PM (PM-3 is interview-only)
    and 80% for SPM (SPM-3 is interview-only).
  - Do not rank candidates directly against each other if they are at different assessment stages,
    or if their coverage differs by more than 15 percentage points (a configurable convention).
  - There are no automatic accept or reject thresholds. Before any score bands are used
    operationally, the founder or a delegated human reviewer should review the bands against worked
    examples.
  - Round the score to one decimal place. Differences of a few points are within the noise of
    subjective anchoring and should not decide outcomes.
